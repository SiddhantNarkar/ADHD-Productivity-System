ADHD Disease Overview

What is Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder ?

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a common condition that affects how a person pays attention, stays focused, and controls their behavior. It usually begins in childhood but can continue into adulthood. People with ADHD may find it hard to sit still, follow instructions, or stay on one task for a long time. It’s important to understand that ADHD is not a sign of laziness or bad behavior - it’s a genuine neurological condition that influences how the brain manages attention, impulse control, and activity levels.

People with ADHD often show a range of symptoms that can differ from person to person. Inattention means having trouble staying focused, missing small details, or getting easily distracted. Hyperactivity shows as constant movement, restlessness, or fidgeting even in calm situations. Impulsivity involves acting before thinking, interrupting others, or struggling to wait one’s turn. Disorganization makes it difficult to manage time, tasks, or responsibilities effectively. Forgetfulness can lead to missing deadlines, appointments, or losing important items. Difficulty completing tasks happens when someone starts projects with energy but finds it hard to finish them. Poor emotional regulation causes quick frustration, mood swings, or feeling easily overwhelmed. Lastly, trouble prioritizing means finding it hard to decide what’s most important, often leading to procrastination. These symptoms can affect learning, work, and daily life, but with awareness, understanding, and support, people with ADHD can find ways to adapt and thrive.
